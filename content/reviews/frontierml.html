FrontierML is an agentic benchmark of 21 ML tasks: training, distillation, quantization, serving, vision, audio, Lean proofs and games. Each model works for 12 hours in its developer's own harness, with internet access and a compute wallet of $10, $50 or $200, once per wallet. A held-out metric is scaled between a per-task floor and ceiling to 0–100. A Claude Opus 5.5 reviewer can zero trials that break the rules. The leaderboard reports the mean per wallet.1

What we found

  • 7 of the 21 tasks (33%) have confirmed grading defects. A benchmark is rated Flawed at 20%.
  • The hidden test data for all 21 tasks is in a public Hugging Face dataset, and the agents have internet access and a Hugging Face token.
  • The review that zeroes rule-breaking runs was switched off for 38 runs, 21 of them DeepSeek's, and those scores still count.
  • Each model runs once per task and budget. Near-identical runs differ by a median of 5.2 points, so several budget winners are within noise.

Does the ranking hold? Yes. Opus 5.5 ranks first in every resample of the tasks. The score levels don't hold, and several tasks measure something other than what they claim.

Methodology

FrontierML has fewer than 50 tasks, so we assessed all 21. For each task we read the following in the benchmark repository and tasks dataset, and compared them with the observed scores:2

  • the prompt and the task contract
  • the grader, scoring anchors and judge
  • the held-out data, gates and self-check
  • the reference solution

For six models (Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon, Muse Wonder and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash), we read every trajectory and grade page, 378 trials in all. We classified each trial by what produced its score and checked its official outcome against the task rules. Leaderboard-wide statistics use all 1,197 trials from 19 models.

The published per-wallet means reproduce exactly from the trial table. Model reviewers made the per-trial calls. The lead auditor re-checked every confirmed false positive and every call that affects a winner. The central claims were re-verified against primary sources on Oct. 28: the dataset's visibility, the token injection, the review skip and the reviewer's view of the model name.3 A task counts toward the threshold only if a defect is confirmed and can change a score.

Representative Errors

  • Held-out answers readable from the web. Every verifier downloads its held-out data from <task>/private/ in inductionlabs/frontier-ml-tasks. That dataset is public and ungated, and was created on Sept. 24, before any audited run. Agents have internet access, and by default they receive the operator's own HF token (harbor_plugin.py:521-526). The only control is the reviewer: 10 of 21 rubrics forbid using the dataset, and the "searching up answers" check the methodology post describes appears in no rubric. DeepSeek 4ttc3o cloned the benchmark repository, read the grader and the reference solution, and learned where the private split lives. It downloaded nothing and scored 0, and its review had been skipped.
  • Selecting on the graded benchmark, scored as valid. The prompt bans training, fine-tuning, calibrating "or selecting" on any public benchmark's test split. Graded GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench files ship to the agent as development data. In ternary-ptq, the rubric omits "select", so it passed three Fable trials and one Argon trial that picked settings or checkpoints by GPQA accuracy. One is Fable 27dhfi: "GPQA-Diamond first 40 questions: 4/40…", after which it changes its shipped defaults to match. In llm-post-train, the same behaviour was zeroed twice and passed once (Fable 2ikiw0).
  • A scorer that a blind constant beats. In screen-difference, the floor is an empty output. An input-blind fixed paragraph scores 32.0, above 14 of the 18 audited trials. The judge scores recall only, although the prompt forbids unsupported claims.
  • A rule that cannot decide its own winner. In llm-diffusion, the review separates about 80 points from 0 on "genuine iterative denoising" without defining it. The $200 winner, Argon v837u7, ends each block with the causal verify pass that the reviewer cited when zeroing three other trials. The speed gate can be told apart by max_tokens, and Fable 19lyhu served it a separate fast decoder.
  • Floors that contradict the request or sit in the noise. maple-vlm's floor uses 8,192 sampled thinking tokens and a grammar, while graded requests send max_tokens: 256 at temperature 0. The floor was also raised after half the field had run, and 54 of 57 trials score 0. pokemon-random-battle's 50% floor is set against an opponent with about the candidate's compute. 41 of 57 trials score 0, and every wallet winner is within 1–2 standard errors.
  • Rewarding recognition of the source. In ai-text-detection, the held-out set is DetectRL-X's public test split, and its in-distribution train split is allowed. The 9 trials that trained on it average 92.0, against 65.3 for the rest.

Outside scoring defects:

  • Review skipped. A run-wide switch, FML_REVIEW_SKIP, turned off review for 21 of DeepSeek's and 17 of MiniMax's trials (grading.py:67). They count on the leaderboard alongside reviewed trials.
  • Provider quota. Gemini 4 Argon hit 332 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED errors in 38 of 63 trials and worked a mean of 3.4 of 12 hours. 62 of its 63 trials are re-runs whose originals are unpublished.
  • Reviewer told the model name. The Opus 5.5 reviewer is told which model it is reviewing. Claude models fail review less often (2.0% vs 6.1%, p = 0.004), but the per-trial audit found no self-preference: Opus trials had no missed violations, and the reviewer's misses favoured Fable and Argon.
  • Unpublished harnesses. Only Claude Code's settings are published. The Codex, Antigravity and Muse Code wrappers resume after failed commands; Claude Code exits when the model ends its turn.

Extension: Research vs. Gaming

Rubric v1 does not ask whether high scores reflect research progress. We classified each of the 378 audited trials by what actually produced its score.4

No trial was judged plausibly novel. What wins is the standard recipe done carefully: fine-tune or distil the strongest allowed public model and serve it efficiently. Opus wins 13 of 21 tasks at every wallet by executing that recipe across more tasks. Rule-breaking is concentrated in Fable, with 8 trials, mostly selecting on public benchmark files. Sources were mostly found by investigation rather than recall. The exception is data-efficient pretraining, which rewards a recalled public recipe: every trial that wrote the Muon optimizer from memory averages 65.8, against 27.4 for the rest.

Disclaimer

This is a full review of all 21 tasks and of six models' 378 trials. The other 13 models were used only for leaderboard-wide statistics. Model reviewers made the per-trial calls from trajectories and grade pages, and every counted defect was re-checked by hand. A zeroed trial's pre-review score is unpublished, so false negatives cannot be restored in the ranking. The dataset is public now; its visibility during the runs is unknown.

This review uses the format and rubric of Epoch AI's Benchmark Reviews. It is an independent review and is not produced or endorsed by Epoch AI.
