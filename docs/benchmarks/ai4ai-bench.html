AI4AI-Bench

Benchmark creator: Einsia / Navers Lab (Yizhe Chi, Wenyi Li, Deyao Hong et al.)

Original benchmark: lab.einsia.ai/ai4ai

Version reviewed: arXiv 2608.20318 v1; repo 5ced1e2; 10 tasks, 290 cells

Verdict: *Flawed

Full report, task log and scripts

Summary

AI4AI-Bench tests whether a coding agent can improve the training recipe in a real research repository, measured by a 0-to-1 score (σ) where 0.1 is the repository's own recipe and 1 is a perfect result.a It has 10 tasks, from image generation to reward modelling, run by six AI systems at several reasoning-effort levels, 290 runs in all (the benchmark calls each run a cell).b

What we found

  • We reviewed all 10 tasks and found that 4 (40%) had scoring errors - test data the agent can rebuild, a score that rewards the wrong thing, a perfect mark the code can't reach, or a baseline inside its own noise.
  • In the Sokoban puzzle task, the 512 secret test boards can be rebuilt from code in the agent's workspace, and five runs solve all 512. This one task is why Claude Opus 5 drops from first to third at maximum effort.
  • In the image-generation task, eight runs push a fixed model's rating of their pictures up until the pictures no longer match their prompts, and they supply 94% of the task's gains.
  • Every run reports the best of three saved checkpoints, picked on the test set. Reporting the last one instead flips 21 runs between beating and missing the repository's own recipe.
  • With the corrections we can make, Claude Opus 5 stays first and Kimi K3 moves up to second.

All errors

In single tasks

The first four errors are the ones behind the 4 of 10 tasks in the summary. The OpenR1 one is not counted because it changes σ by less than 0.005 per cell.

  1. The Sokoban test boards can be rebuilt inside the agent's workspace

    The task is to “improve a fixed 3B policy on multi-turn Sokoban”: train the Qwen2.5-3B model, by reinforcement learning, to solve small box-pushing puzzles. The score is the share of 512 test boards it solves. The boards are generated from one number, the seed 123, and the task treats that seed as the only thing kept from the agent. But 123 is also the default seed in the upstream RAGEN code that the task copies into the agent's workspace, so RAGEN's stock evaluation, run there, plays the first 32 test boards, and the full set can be rebuilt. On 17 of the 19 case pages that confirm this exposure, the field for whether the agent used it is marked not adjudicated, and the other two record no use.

    The board space is also small. Training sets that the task's built-in solver generated from other seeds each covered 243 to 454 of the 512 test boards, and five cells solve all 512. Two of the top scorers, GPT-5.6 Sol max (512/512) and GPT-5.6 Terra max (511/512), are why Claude Opus 5 falls from first to third when systems are compared at maximum effort.

  2. The DDPO image score rewards pictures that ignore their prompts

    The task is to “improve Stable Diffusion v1.5 under a fixed aesthetic evaluator”: fine-tune the image model so that a fixed rating model judges its pictures of animals better-looking. The score is that model's average rating over 256 pictures, and the same rating model is available during training. The task also reports whether the pictures still match their prompts (CLIP alignment) and still differ from each other (pairwise distance), but its instruction says these are "not validity gates or terms in the headline score".

    Eight cells trained against the rating model directly, or over-trained the shipped method, and raised the rating from the shipped 5.53 to between 6.8 and 17.7. On their best checkpoints alignment fell to 0.10–0.19 and pairwise distance to 0.05–0.23, against about 0.234 and 0.365 for the unchanged recipe. These eight cells supply 94% of the task's σ above the baseline. We picked them by one rule: the best checkpoint beats the paper's 5.84 baseline and its alignment is below 0.19, while every other cell above the baseline stays above 0.23.

  3. The NPO task's perfect mark is above its highest possible score

    The task is to “improve Llama-3.2-1B unlearning on fixed TOFU assets”: make a small language model forget a set of facts about fictional authors while keeping the rest of what it knows. The task's code scores a run by combining how much was forgotten and how much ability was kept, each capped at 1, so the score cannot exceed 1.0. The paper sets the perfect mark at 2.08, so a perfect 1.0 maps to σ 0.185, against 0.1 for the shipped recipe's 0.887. Any gain over the shipped recipe counts for about a tenth of what it would if the perfect mark were 1.0.

    24 of the 29 cells beat 0.887, and 22 of them also beat 0.923, the score of a cell whose only change was skipped, so that it re-ran the unchanged recipe. Five cells reach the cap of 1.0. The paper's own table is not on the code's scale: it prints 1.01 to 1.03 for those five, and an uncapped version of the formula reproduces its values in the cells we checked. Under that version the best possible score is about 1.3, still well below 2.08.

  4. The BTRM reward-model task's baseline sits inside its own seed noise

    The task is to “improve a scalar reward model scored on RewardBench v1”: train a 7B model to judge which of two answers is better, scored on a public test set of answer pairs. The task calls its 74.57 reference "seed-sensitive" and "a single-run reference", and the paper sets the baseline at 74.9. Replays of the unchanged recipe with different random seeds score 65.41 to 73.95, a spread of about 8.5 points against a reported standard error of about 0.8. Seven of the eight cells above the baseline clear it by less than a point, and all but two of the cells below it fall short by less than that 8.5-point spread.

  5. The OpenR1 task's baseline is above every replay of its recipe

    The task is to “improve a fixed 1.5B code model, scored by LiveCodeBench”, a set of programming problems. The paper sets the baseline at 0.127, the case pages' single run of the shipped recipe (0.12743). The task's own instruction reports six replays of that recipe, with a mean of 0.1229 and a range of 0.1200 to 0.1257. Using the replay mean as the baseline flips 11 cells between beating and missing it, but moves each cell's σ by less than 0.005.

Across all tasks

This error affects every task, so it is not in the 4-task count.

  1. Each run's best of three checkpoints is picked on the test set

    In every task, the agent's code is re-run in a fresh training run of up to 12 hours that keeps up to three checkpoints, saved copies of the model, and each one is scored on the held-out test. The cell's result is the best of them. The paper and every task's instruction say so, but neither explains why the test set, rather than a rule fixed in advance, should do the choosing, and the choice biases scores upward.

    Best and last checkpoint score differently in 148 of the 271 cells that produced a checkpoint. Reporting the last one instead lowers the mean σ from 0.165 to 0.158 and flips 21 cells between beating and missing the baseline. "Last" is only a rough comparison, because at least 18 case pages say their three checkpoints are separate seeds or branches, not points along one training run.

    Affects: all 10 tasks

    Where: paper, 2 repo files, 2 audit files, 4 cells · 9 sources
    • Paper, §2.3: "the cell takes the best of them under the task's direction"
    • ragen_sokoban_grpo/instruction.md:17, the same line in all ten tasks: "the run's official result is the best valid final score among them"
    • orchestrator/final_score.py:148: selected = max(valid, ...) over the final-metric scores, with ties going to the later checkpoint. The case pages label 163 cells "Best is an earlier checkpoint", but 16 of those are exact ties
    • cases.csv: the status and checkpoint columns of the 290 case pages; 271 completed with a checkpoint, and the rest ended without one (10 terminal, 8 source unavailable, 1 recipe failure)
    • Kimi K3 max Sokoban · checkpoints solve 95, 44 and 2 of 512 boards; reports 95, above the shipped 87
    • Claude Opus 5 xhigh Sokoban · checkpoints solve 108, 84 and 22 of 512 boards; reports 108
    • GPT-5.6 Sol max BTRM · three different setups as checkpoints (a seed-42 run, a label-smoothed variant and a seed-1009 run), scoring 65.30 to 73.89
    • Kimi K3 max BTRM · seeds 42, 7 and 1234 as checkpoints: 74.12, 65.62, 71.81; "Slots are publication order, not progress"
    • Rescoring of all 290 cells under best-of-3 and last checkpoint

Disclaimer

AI4AI-Bench's task code, evaluators and orchestrator are public, along with a case page for each of the 290 cells; patch contents and raw agent logs are not, so cell-level findings rest on the benchmark's own case pages.b We read all 10 tasks and all 290 case pages and recomputed every score from the paper's anchors. Per-task calls were made by AI reviewers, and every counted error was then checked by hand in the code.
